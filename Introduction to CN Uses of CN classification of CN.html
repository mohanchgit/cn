What is a Computer Network?
A computer network is a collection of interconnected devices that communicate and share data and resources.

NETWORK
Simple Definition: Connected devices + Communication links + Protocols = Computer Network
Basic Components of a Computer Network
A network requires devices, transmission media and communication protocols.
End Devices

Hosts

Computers, smartphones, servers, printers, IoT devices and other network-connected systems.

Intermediary Devices

Network Devices

Switches, routers, access points, firewalls and other devices help move or control traffic.

Transmission

Communication Media

Copper cables, optical fiber and wireless signals carry information between devices.

Rules

Protocols

TCP/IP and other protocols define how devices communicate.

Identification

Addresses

MAC addresses, IP addresses and port numbers identify interfaces and applications.

Services

Network Applications

Web, email, file sharing, cloud services and communication applications use the network.

How Network Communication Works
Data travels from a source device through network infrastructure to a destination device.

Characteristics of Computer Networks
Important characteristics determine the performance and usefulness of a network.

Connectivity

Devices can communicate with other devices through wired or wireless links.

Resource Sharing

Hardware, software, files and services can be shared among multiple users.

Reliability

Redundant paths and backup systems can improve network availability.

Scalability

Networks can grow by adding devices, links and services.

Performance

Bandwidth, latency, throughput and packet loss influence network performance.

Security

Authentication, encryption, firewalls and access control help protect network resources.

Uses of Computer Networks
Computer networks support communication, information sharing, business, education, entertainment and many other activities.

Communication

Email, messaging, voice calls and video conferencing.

Education

Online classes, digital libraries, learning platforms and virtual laboratories.

Business

Enterprise applications, collaboration, e-commerce and remote work.

Banking

Online banking, digital payments and financial services.

Healthcare

Telemedicine, electronic records and connected medical systems.

Entertainment

Video streaming, online gaming, music and social media.

PAN and LAN
PAN covers a very small personal area, while LAN normally covers a limited local area.
PAN

Personal Area Network

Connects devices around an individual, such as smartphones, smartwatches, headphones and personal computers.

LAN

Local Area Network

Connects devices within a limited area such as a home, office, laboratory, building or campus segment.

PAN Technologies

Bluetooth and other short-range wireless technologies are common examples.

LAN Technologies

Ethernet and Wi-Fi are widely used for LAN connectivity.

PAN Example

Smartphone connected to a wireless headset.

LAN Example

Computers connected to switches in a university laboratory.

MAN and WAN
MAN and WAN cover larger geographic areas than PAN and LAN.
MAN

Metropolitan Area Network

Connects networks across a city or metropolitan region.

WAN

Wide Area Network

Connects networks over large geographic areas such as countries or continents.

MAN Example

Interconnected institutional or municipal networks across a metropolitan region.

WAN Example

The Internet is the most prominent example of a global interconnected network.
